A PRACTICAL START FOR PARENTS

How to set up parental controls on a child’s iPhone or iPad.

Start with Apple’s family tools. Add device restrictions only when you need the extra controls and understand the setup involved.

Download the one-page checklist ↓

Your starting point: Give your child their own Apple Account, connect it to Family Sharing, and configure Screen Time from your parent device. A Mac app or supervision isn’t required for these Apple features.

1. Set up your child’s account and iCloud email

Already have an Apple Account for your child? Keep that account and skip to step 2. Only follow step 1 if they need an account of their own.

Start with your child’s own Apple Account and, if you choose, an @icloud.com email address. On your parent iPhone or iPad, go to Settings → your name → Family. Set up the family group if prompted, then choose Add Member → Create Child Account. Enter your child’s correct birthday and follow Apple’s prompts.

Apple confirms that a Child Account enables age-based safeguards, and that an incorrect birthday may prevent some protections from being enabled. Review the age-appropriate settings during setup. Defaults and available safeguards depend on software, age, and region.

Apple: child account setup · Apple: connect your family · Apple: age-based safeguards

Give them an email address they can grow with

When creating your child’s account, consider choosing an @icloud.com email address. Setting it up now gives them their own address to keep using as they get older, and avoids relying on a parent’s inbox. Choose something that will still suit them in their teens. You can use their account for family controls now and introduce sending and receiving email when they’re ready.

An Apple Account and an iCloud email address aren’t quite the same thing: an existing account can use another email provider. If your child already has an Apple Account, keep it and follow Apple’s instructions to create an iCloud Mail address rather than making a duplicate account.

Apple: create an iCloud Mail address

2. Connect their account and set up Screen Time

First, check that your child appears in Settings → Family on your parent device. Accounts created through Family Sharing are added during setup. For an existing account that isn’t listed, set up your family group if needed, then choose Add Member → Invite Others and follow Apple’s instructions. Make sure your child’s iPhone or iPad is signed in to their own account.

From the parent or guardian’s device, open Settings → Family → your child → Screen Time. Follow the setup prompts and review content access, usage routines, and communication settings available for your family. Protect the settings with a Screen Time passcode your child doesn’t know.

Keep family devices updated. Apple’s current guidance describes a newer Screen Time experience on version 27 and a previous experience for devices that cannot update. Follow the options actually shown on your devices rather than assuming every menu or feature is identical.

Apple: manage your child’s Screen Time

3. Review app approvals and time requests

Consider allowing app installation while using Ask to Buy for eligible downloads and purchases. That leaves room to approve something new without rebuilding a device profile each time. Review an app’s content, purchases, contacts, and privacy choices with your child.

Screen Time requests for extra time and Ask to Buy requests for eligible purchases or downloads are separate. On supported versions, parents can respond in Messages. In Apple’s newer controls, open your child’s family settings → Apps & Websites → Restrictions → Ask to Buy to review Require Purchase Approval. Menus differ on earlier versions.

4. Test the experience on both devices

  • Confirm the child’s device uses their own account and that the account appears in your family group.
  • Try one content or usage limit together and check what happens on the child’s screen.
  • Try an eligible app request and a Screen Time request for more time. Check that the parent can receive and respond to each. Make sure a parent or guardian is in the child’s contacts.
  • Keep essential communication available and discuss what to do when something feels wrong.
  • Repeat the checks after meaningful software or account changes.

If a control doesn’t behave as expected, revisit Apple’s current instructions and account setup. Family Sharing provides a foundation for management, not a promise of flawless enforcement.

When might you want device supervision?

If your family needs additional supported device restrictions, Little Boundaries is a Mac app in development that builds profiles and guides the supervision workflow. It does not provide remote monitoring or a replacement for Apple’s family controls.

Initial supervision in this workflow erases an unsupervised device.

Have a backup and a recovery plan first. Restoring an older backup can affect supervision. The app requires explicit confirmation before erasing. Physical-device validation and a public signed, notarized release are still pending.

5. Set up parental controls inside the apps

Once the device boundaries are in place, review the apps your child uses. Social apps and games may have their own family linking, privacy, messaging, content, and spending controls. Little Boundaries does not configure those account settings for you.

Keep the conversation going

Explain why you chose each limit, make room for exceptions, and revisit the choices as your child grows. Controls can reduce risk, but they cannot guarantee safety or replace attention and conversation.
